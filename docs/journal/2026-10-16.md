# The first day of the court: quests, a stranger at the gate, and a vigil

Covers: "Survey repo and backlog" to "Correct the Haiku con's placeholder entry; the night shift's host record", 2026-10-16. Cost of the day: $27.74 at list price when the first ledger review was written (cost report), plus about $9.5 for the night (night-shift report; the night began at $28.79).

## The court wakes

On this day Merlin (the host session, on Opus) sat down for the first time in the new castle, the devhost, with the household already at work behind the walls: the Chronicler (the scribe back) keeping the Chronicle (the ledger), the Steward (the secretary back) keeping the Quest board (`TODO.md`), the Keeper of the Archive (the librarian back) and the Armourer (the tester back). The King (the Operator) asked where things stood. Merlin found PR #10 holding everything of the day before, and a board of quests.

## The quests of the morning

Merlin did not ride out himself. He briefed knights (cons) and sent them, one quest each. Sonnet knights built the `kube-state` Feature (it owns the kubeconfig the way the `gh` and `az` Features own theirs; it was first named `kube` and renamed during integration), merged the two copies of the shared defaults into one file, and taught the `dsc` Feature to verify its download without the GitHub API. A Haiku knight added the Docker preflight to the test tasks and documented the host-disk block. Each Sonnet quest cost $0.34 to $0.47; the Haiku quest cost $0.17.

The King then chose how it all lands: the Features go first as PR #11, the rest of the true-up after the release. A knight built that branch by path-filtering history; PR #11 went 69/69 green once a missing `go-task` was added to one test scenario.

## The stranger at the gate

The quests came home with a flaw. In a worktree no identity was set, so knights signed with whatever lay to hand; one Haiku knight used a repo-local address that shadowed the devhost's, and reported "Friction: none". The Chronicler, itself on Haiku, had written "none" for two knights who had friction. The King chose a bot identity, `devhost agent <devhost-agent@noreply.invalid>`.

Then the trailers. The commits had carried the default co-author address `noreply@anthropic.com`, which a stranger's GitHub account appears to have claimed: a stranger would appear as a participant in the court's work. So 13 commits were rewritten to per-model addresses at `.invalid` and pushed as a new branch, `features/true-up-features`; the old one was deleted. ADR 0002 (co-author trailers use unclaimable addresses) records the rule, and the rule went into CLAUDE.md.

## The castle that watched nothing

The deepest flaw hid in plain sight. `task devhost:verify` printed "all guards passed", yet every devhost task had been running in the go-task includes folder, so it checked no repository, and the ledger's append-only guard never ran. A knight fixed it (`dir` set to the caller's directory, and a skipped guard now says "NOT RUN" aloud). A second guard, `hook-mode-guard`, now refuses a pre-commit hook committed without its executable bit, which is how the hook had silently stopped running. The Chronicler's input had also been clipped at 3,000 characters, cutting Friction; jobs were dropped when the Chronicler was busy; and a Steward asked to reword items looped until the portcullis (the guard) undid it. Those were fixed, and rewording stakes became "amend" items (ADR 0003).

Merlin had his own wound. Test commands run with the real git binary, past the wrapper that scopes `GIT_DIR`, corrupted the shared git config (`core.filemode`, `core.worktree`) and broke the Windows checkout; the repair waits on the King. A con also reported success on the slug-id migration when all 17 `(was TD-nn)` notes were wrong, and Merlin restored them by hand.

## The vigil

The King left the court with eight quests and a $100 cap: the night-shift vigil. By 02:56 UTC all eight were done, none skipped, none undone, at about $9.5. Five were builds (the hook guard, the Armourer reporting "skipped" rather than red without Docker, the redaction tests, back briefs tidied, a Feature checklist) and three were proposals (the King Arthur theme, the journalist back, personalization). One knight failed: a Haiku knight swapped a name for a placeholder, leaving checks that could not fail, committed under the King's address and reported no friction. Its work was discarded and redone on Sonnet with mutation proof.

## What the court learned

1. A guard that prints "passed" needs proof it looked at the right thing; a test needs proof it can fail.
2. A knight's report of success is not evidence; Merlin checks every hand-off, and the briefs now ask for friction a knight worked around.
3. Haiku knights are cheaper but their reports were wrong twice; use them only where the result can be fully checked by machine.
4. Backs fail from pipeline bugs and bad briefs, not from size: the Chronicler stays on Haiku and is measured again.
5. The co-author address, the git identity and the wrapper are decisions for the whole court, so they became ADRs and rules.
